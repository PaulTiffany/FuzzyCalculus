# 6. Smooth Enough for Whom?

Little Bear drew a staircase. From far away, Sugar Bear thought it looked like a ramp.

“It is smooth,” said Sugar Bear.

“It has corners,” said Little Bear, standing close.

Again, both bears were honest.

Something is **smooth enough for observer \(O\)** when all the differences that observer checks are smaller than its resolution \(\epsilon_O\).

In grown-up shorthand:

\[
\lVert\delta_O^n(\text{next}-\text{now})\rVert < \epsilon_O
\]

The long expression says something gentle:

> At every level of change this observer checks, nothing jumps more than the observer can notice.

Smoothness is therefore not always a property of the staircase alone. It can be a relationship among the staircase, the observer, the viewing distance, and the question being asked.

## Tiny lesson

“Smooth” may mean “no roughness exists,” or it may mean “no roughness is visible here.” Fuzzy calculus keeps those claims apart.
