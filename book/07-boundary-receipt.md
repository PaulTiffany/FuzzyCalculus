# 7. The Boundary Keeps the Receipt

Sugar Bear walked along a path:

\[
2\rightarrow5\rightarrow4\rightarrow9
\]

The little changes were:

\[
+3,\;-1,\;+5
\]

Add them:

\[
3-1+5=7
\]

Now compare only the end and the beginning:

\[
9-2=7
\]

The inside steps canceled in just the right way. The boundary—the beginning and end—kept the receipt for the whole trip.

This is a tiny version of a great calculus idea: when local changes fit together properly, their total can be read at the boundary.

But fuzzy calculus asks one more question:

> Did anything become too small, blurry, or hidden for our observer while the steps were added?

A perfect total does not always prove that every inside step was perfect.

## Tiny lesson

Boundaries can summarize a journey, but a summary may hide what happened inside.

## Honey check

If you leave home with 2 cookies and return with 2 cookies, does the boundary prove you never traded or ate any along the way?
