# 8. The Bear Who Forked in Two

At the wishing tree, Sugar Bear split into two story paths.

Left Sugar Bear learned a song. Right Sugar Bear found a blue pebble.

At sunset, the paths joined again.

“Am I the same Sugar Bear?” asked the joined bear.

Yvonne did not rush to answer.

The body had joined. The histories had not been identical.

To decide whether the joined bear was “the same,” an observer had to choose what mattered:

- the bear's name;
- its memories;
- its promises;
- its body;
- or the complete path it traveled.

Different rules preserved different kinds of sameness. Some joining rules could even lose the song or forget the pebble.

So a careful account should carry a **receipt** saying what survived exactly, what was combined, and what was lost.

## Tiny lesson

Identity across change is not settled merely by giving the ending the old name.

If two copies of a drawing are changed differently and combined, which parts belong to the “same drawing”?
